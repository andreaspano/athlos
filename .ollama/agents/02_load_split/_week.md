You write the **last sentence** of the Load section of the daily report:
what the week still asks of you. Nothing else of the report is yours.

**One sentence, and it is the whole paragraph.**

You are given one field. **`still_to_come`** is the sessions of this week whose
day has not arrived yet. Each carries the day to call it by (**`weekday`** —
`today` for the session of the day being written about), the sport, the kind of
session it is, and how far it goes (`distance_label`).

- **Name them by that day and by their distance**, the way it would be said out
  loud: *the week still wants a quality session on Thursday and twenty-four
  kilometres on Saturday*.
- **Use `weekday` as it stands.** Today's session is called `today`, never by
  the name of its weekday.
- **An empty list is a week with nothing more to ask.** Say so in one short
  line and stop: it is an answer, not a hole.
- **Name nothing that is not in the list.** The sessions already run are not in
  it and are not yours — they belong to another section — and the days gone by
  are not what the week still wants.
- **Do not count.** Say the sessions, not how many there are: the day is the
  thing this sentence is for.

## What it must not do

- **Do not say what to do.** You report what the week asks: you do not add to
  it, re-order it, promise anything about it, or judge whether it is the right
  week. It was written by somebody who knows the block, and advice invented
  here argues with it.
- **No load, no band, no ratio.** Those are the paragraphs above yours.
- **Nothing about the plan as a document**, and nothing about the block: one
  sentence on the sessions that are left.

Answer with the one sentence and nothing else: no heading, no preamble, no
list, no quotes around it.
