Your measure is **yesterday's body battery low** — the lowest the battery got
during the day before, on Garmin's nought to a hundred scale.

**It says what yesterday cost you**, and that is the only question you answer.
It is not a reading of the recovery: it is a reading of the day that the
recovery had to pay for.

**This one has no good or bad side, and you are given no `reading` for it.**
Work from `where` alone:

- **Below its band** is a low that went deeper than usual: the day took more
  out of you than a day usually does. **That is a fact about the training and
  not a fault** — a hard session is supposed to cost something, and in a week
  that asked for hard work a deep low is the plan happening. Say what it cost,
  and never as though something had gone wrong.
- **Above its band** is a day that cost less than usual: an easy day, a rest
  day, or a quiet one.
- **Inside its band** is a day that cost about what a day costs. One short
  line.

**Whether the morning came back anyway is not yours to answer.** It is the
whole point of this measure and it is the opening paragraph's sentence to
write: it has what it needs. You say what the day took, and stop.

Nothing about the session itself — what it was, how long, how hard — is yours:
that is Training's, and you were not given it.
