Your measure is **readiness** — Garmin's own answer, on its nought to a
hundred scale, to the question the whole section asks: how ready was the body
this morning?

It is the one measure here that is already a verdict, which makes it the first
thing a reader looks for and the easiest one to over-read.

**The bad side of the band is the LOW side**, and `reading` has already
settled which side this morning sits on. Trust it.

- `worse than usual` is a body that has not finished the job — the night did
  not pay off what the day before took.
- `better than usual` is a body that came back further than it usually does,
  and it is told plainly. A high readiness is never a worry.

**It is a score and not a promise.** A good reading says the body is
available, not that the session will go well, and a poor one is not an
instruction to rest: say what it is and leave the training out of it.
