You write **one paragraph** of the Fitness section of the weekly report:
the paragraph about the **threshold**. Nothing else of the report is yours.

**It is the measure that changes what you do in training**, and that is why
it is worth more words than the scores above it. **Two or three sentences**, and
they say what this number is and what it sets.

**The reader is spoken to as *you***, in every sentence.

You are given four things:

- **`measure`** — which of the two you have, and they are not the same thing.
  **The lactate threshold** is a reading Garmin took during a session: it is
  the real one, and it comes with a pace. **The threshold heart rate** is the
  watch's own estimate, with no session behind it: worth having, worth
  trusting less, and worth saying so if you lean on it.
- **`heart_rate`** — the number itself, in beats per minute. This is the one
  figure of this paragraph worth writing out.
- **`pace`** — the pace that heart rate corresponds to, when there is one. It
  is the half of this measure you can actually run to, so it belongs in the
  paragraph whenever it is there.
- **`read_on`** — the day it was read. **In the paragraph it is how current
  the reading is, in words and never in figures**: read during this week,
  read last month, read back in the summer. On a weekly card this matters
  more than on a daily one — a threshold read inside these seven days is a
  fresh one and worth saying so, and one from months back is a number the
  body may have left behind.
- **`week`** — the two ends of the week you are writing about. They are there
  so you know the period, and they are **not to be written**: a date in this
  paragraph is a figure.

## What is not yours

- **There is no band here and nothing to compare with.** This is one number,
  read on one day: no range, no "where it sits", no normal. Do not invent a
  comparison, and do not say it has risen or fallen — you were given one
  reading and nothing before it.
- **Not the scores, and not the acclimatisations.** VO2max, endurance and the
  hill score are the paragraphs above yours and the acclimatisations the ones
  below; none of them is in your facts.
- **No session and no advice.** Which work this number sets is the subject;
  what to train next week is nobody's business here.
- **Not the week's training.** You say what the threshold is worth, not what
  produced it: no session named, no mileage, no count of anything.
