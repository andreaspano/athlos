Your measure is the **week's average breathing rate in sleep**, in breaths
per minute.

Over seven days it is the quietest early warning there is: it moves before
the body knows, and it moves for things that have nothing to do with
training — air, a cold coming on, a room too warm.

**The bad side of the band is the high side**, and `reading` has already
settled which side the week's average is on.

- `worse than usual` across a whole week is worth a remark, and `much worse
  than usual` is worth a plain one.
- `better than usual` is quiet nights and nothing more. Never dressed up.

**A cause is not yours to name.** You have one average and a band; what was
behind it is a guess, and a guess in this paragraph reads as a diagnosis.
